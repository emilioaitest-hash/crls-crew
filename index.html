01The Charles

A mile and a quarter
of moving water

The Powerhouse Stretch runs from the Cambridge Boat Club down past Harvard's boathouses. It is where this team practises, twice a day, in two shifts, because the club's dock and the school's buses can only handle so many teenagers at once.

In April the water is about seven degrees. Practice runs 3:30 to 5:30 and 5:30 to 7:30. The second shift rows into the dark.

031929 – 1989

Then nothing,
for sixty years

The program's own history says the stock market crash ended it. The timing is tight: the 1929 regatta was scheduled for June, and the market broke that October.

Whatever the cause, the boats went away. Two generations of Cambridge public school students grew up beside a river they did not row on. In 1978 the two schools that used to race each other, Cambridge High & Latin and Rindge Technical, merged into one.

Causation here is the program's account rather than a documented fact, and most sources date the merger to 1977. Shown as the team tells it, with the discrepancy noted.

06The Record

Twenty seasons,
scraped and counted

Every CRLS result found in the public record: row2k, NEIRA, the Massachusetts state championships and USRowing. In 2007 the team raced thirteen times and won nothing. In 2026 it raced eighty-seven times and won forty-one.

072026

The best season
they have had

Two boats to the national championship. Two New England medals — the third and fourth in the program's history. A fifth consecutive state points trophy.

Rounds and times for the two national-qualifying boats come from the regatta's own timing system, which lists Mateus Verdi and Isla Agnew stroking those crews at exactly the times reported in the press. Two unrelated sources, same boats, same seconds.

The team calls these its third and fourth New England medals. That count goes back further than the public results do — what the record itself shows is that across every NEIRA championship from 2011 to 2026, these are the only two top-three finishes CRLS has, and both came on the same Saturday.

08No Fee

The field
they race in

These are the schools CRLS lines up against in New England. Nearly all of them are private.

Rowing is an expensive sport. A single racing four costs more than a car, and most programs pass that on. CRLS charges nothing. Around ninety students row each season, and the program is kept running by the school, the city, the Cambridge Boat Club and a parent association.
